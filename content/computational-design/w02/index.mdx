DesComp 2’s role-card exercise gave me a structure for describing purpose, inputs, behavior, knowledge, and boundaries.

Our group's letter-writing exercise turned a complaint about Berkeley weather into a formal letter to the “God of Weather.” The whiteboard preserved the user's concerns about rain, cold mornings, hot afternoons, and difficulty choosing an outfit, followed by a request for more sunshine and less rain.

In the October 22 class, we acted out the interaction and wrote the letter on the whiteboard.

<Entry date="2026-10-22" title="Observations · Listening While Writing" kind="Classroom observations · October 22">

The observation notes describe empathy, creativity, and a recipient that made the interaction fun. They also capture a difficulty: the user continued adding details while the writer was trying to remember the information and organize a formal letter. At points, the agent asked for clarification.

These observations informed the Empathetic Complaint Ghostwriter role: listen without unnecessary interruption, clarify essential gaps, and preserve the user's facts while giving the complaint a witty formal structure.

<Entry date="2026-10-27" title="System Design · Listen, Clarify, Draft" kind="Role design">

The role needs to hold new details while organizing the letter. I separated behavioral rules from reference knowledge: instructions govern when to ask and how to draft; knowledge supplies the letter structure and possible recipient framing. General Berkeley weather examples must not become personal experiences.

The intended loop is to listen, identify the recipient, clarify missing context, draft, and deliver the text. The user supplies the experience and desired tone. The agent produces correspondence and does not send it or change the real-world situation.

<Section title="Reflection · Listening Is Part of the System" lead>

The playful recipient made the interaction engaging, while the observation notes exposed the difficulty of remembering additional details during drafting. I translated these observations into explicit listening and clarification rules.

The next step is to test those rules with a repeatable script, including a user who asks the agent to proceed before giving enough information.
