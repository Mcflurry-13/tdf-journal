<Entry date="TBD" title="Intent · Reuse What I Save" kind="Design exploration">

I save useful posts, design cases, and portfolio advice across Xiaohongshu and other websites. My folders already have purposes, such as Resume, Portfolio, and UI/UX. I want these materials to become useful again when I begin a creative task, including when I work with an AI assistant.

I initially considered transferring conversation memories between AI tools. Through discussion, I shifted toward a personal inspiration archive: keeping the resources I choose and helping me bring them into a new task.

<Entry date="TBD" title="References · A Library I Can Use" kind="Reference study">

The collection-to-AI workflow in [Material](https://material.sc/guide) helped me clarify the direction. Its public guide describes saving material, organizing it in Spaces, and accessing it through AI tools. This is a reference for the workflow, rather than evidence that my idea is a unique product category.

I want to focus my prototype on a specific moment: returning with a design task and deciding which saved materials are useful. I also decided against proactive recommendations and notifications because I do not want another stream of content to maintain.

<Entry date="TBD" title="Journey · Return With a Task" kind="Proposed scenario">

For a first scenario, I am preparing a portfolio case study and need references for structuring the story. I select my Portfolio archive and explain the task. The agent returns a small set of references, explains their relevance, and suggests what I could learn from each one. I choose what to carry into my next AI conversation.

This is a proposed journey, not an observed user test. Importing an entire social-platform account and directly connecting every AI tool are outside the first experiment; a small manually supplied collection is enough to test the central behavior.

<Entry date="TBD" title="System · From Archive to Context" kind="Proposed architecture">

The proposed system keeps original sources and user notes in an archive. A retrieval step selects candidate materials within the chosen scope; the agent then explains their relevance to the task. The user reviews the results before exporting selected references.

This diagram describes intended responsibilities. The storage, retrieval method, model, and external AI integration have not been implemented or selected yet.

<Entry date="TBD" title="Evaluation · What I Will Test" kind="Test plan">

I will use the same small collection and repeat the same prompts across revisions. For each run, I will keep the instructions and actual output, then note whether the sources are traceable, the selection helps the task, and the suggestions are specific enough to act on.

The first comparison will examine how much context the agent needs: titles and tags alone, then source content and any notes I actually supplied. Further changes will respond to observed failures. These are planned comparisons; the required three rounds of modification and testing are still to be completed.

<Section title="Reflection · A Smaller First Prototype" lead>

The discussion helped me narrow the project to the moment when a collection becomes useful again. Before adding more import formats or AI integrations, I need to find out whether the agent can help me choose a reference and understand how to use it.
