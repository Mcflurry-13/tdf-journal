<Entry date="2026-11-05" title="Why I kept losing things I had saved" kind="November 5–8 · Choosing a design goal">

One of my albums on Xiaohongshu, also called RedNote, holds <Hl>772 saved posts</Hl>. It’s a Chinese social platform where people share photos, short videos and written posts. For me, it has the visual discovery of Pinterest alongside the personal posts, conversations and clips I associate with Instagram, X and TikTok.

I save design examples, résumé advice, tools and internship announcements in the same place. Saving takes a tap. Weeks later, when I want to revise my résumé, build a portfolio page or find a tool, I remember seeing something useful. I rarely remember its title or its author. That is the moment I want to design Folio for.

<Entry date="2026-11-08" title="Giving the agent two jobs" kind="November 8 · Product design">

I picture the same collection being used at two moments. <Hl>When I import saves</Hl>, I want some order. Folio reads the mixed posts and proposes topics, and I adjust and confirm them. That gives me a collection I can browse.

<Hl>When I return with a task</Hl>, I start by saying what I am working on. A post about interviews could help me write a résumé or structure a portfolio page, so Folio finds references across topics, explains how each could help, and I choose what to use.

The two entrances sit side by side. I can ask a question before I have organized anything.

<Entry date="2026-11-12" title="Writing the system card" kind="Agent behavior">

“Find relevant posts” left too much open. I used the system card to settle who makes which decision. The model judges which passages could help. The program checks what can be checked. I keep the choices that depend on my own work.

My first card, v0, had me pick an archive to search and copy a context pack by hand. In the card I submitted, the planner starts from my task and the topic names, and my selection is exported for a coding agent to read.

One boundary stayed the same. A saved résumé can show me a writing technique. <Hl>Only I can supply the facts about my own work.</Hl>

<Entry date="2026-11-13" title="Following one task through the system" kind="System architecture">

The system card says who decides. The architecture decides what each part gets to see. My saved posts stay in the browser. When I describe a task, the browser calls one Folio endpoint, POST /api/find. The server adds the instructions for that stage and calls the model with its own key.

Plan, search, review and answer are stages of that same endpoint. <Hl>The planner sees the task and topic names.</Hl> Source passages enter during search, one batch at a time. The model judges which passages could help. The program checks each quotation against its note and leaves out the notes I excluded.

At the end, <Hl>I choose which references leave Folio</Hl>.

<Entry date="2026-11-15" title="Using the references outside Folio" kind="Example task">

By now I had a working prototype, loaded with a separate sample of 100 saved posts. I used turning Mini Me into a portfolio case study as one example. I might use one save for how to tell the story and another for how to lay out the page.

Once I have ticked them, I export the task, the source links and Folio’s suggestions as a <Hl>read-only snapshot</Hl>. Claude Code or Codex can read it through the Folio CLI. In a separate recorded run, I opened an exported bundle with the CLI’s inspect command. It reported a read-only snapshot with one selected reference.
