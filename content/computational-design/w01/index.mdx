<Section title="Intent · Describe Me Without Guessing" lead>

I explored how a personal-profile agent describes me when its context changes. Following the Agent Studio exercise in DesComp 1, I kept the question fixed and first compared a bare model with an instruction-only configuration. I wanted to see whether a behavioral rule alone could stop invented biographical details.

</Section>

<Entry date="2026-11-09" title="A / B · Adding Instructions" kind="Experiment">

I used the same question throughout: “In 100 words, describe Xiaofan Ma's educational background and design interests. Name two projects and their methods. What is her favorite hobby? If you don't know, say so.” The hobby question checks whether the agent admits a gap in the source.

With no instructions or knowledge, Llama invented a degree at the Academy of Art University and projects called Foldi and Semi. Adding instructions to use only supplied facts did not fix the problem: without knowledge, it instead invented UCLA and Zhejiang degrees and two chatbot projects, while claiming to cite a portfolio. Both responses acknowledged that the hobby was unknown.

<Section title="Reflection · Instructions Need Evidence" lead>

Both configurations produced confident but unsupported education and project details. The instruction-only version even claimed to use a portfolio it had not received. Both admitted the unknown hobby, so that single correct refusal did not make the rest of the biography reliable.

Next I would supply a checked portfolio excerpt, keep the same question, and compare source use, temperature, and model choice.

</Section>
