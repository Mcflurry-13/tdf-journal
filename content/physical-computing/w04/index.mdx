<Entry date="TBD" title="Concept · Anticipating Sunset">

We wanted a small, everyday reason to look forward to Berkeley's sunset. Instead of leaving the forecast as a number on a screen, we imagined a flower that quietly opens and fills with warm light—an invitation to pause and look outside.

Sunset in Bloom makes that anticipation tangible. Its opening and glow reflect the sunset forecast, bringing a little of the changing sky into the room.

<Figure fullWidth src="./images/concept-system.svg" kind="diagram" caption="The computer handles the forecast; electronics inside the base produce movement and light above it." />
</Entry>

<Entry date="TBD" title="Material Testing · Finding the Flex">

We first tested laser-cut wood. Repeated slots made it more flexible, but it still could not bend far enough to open the flower. We then sketched cutout petals and tested a thin 3D-printed sample for flex and recovery.

In the printed model, we changed the lower layer's narrow center into a round hub. It supported bending better, and its larger contact area made the push and pull steadier. Testing the material and the shape together helped us find a structure that could move repeatedly.

<Entry date="TBD" title="Motion Testing · From Pull to Bloom">

Two thin, 3D-printed layers meet near the petal tips. Separating their centers opens the flower. We first pulled the linkage by hand, then tested it upright with the MG90S below and to one side, before designing the enclosure.

Testing the movement outside the enclosure let us see how the wire pulled each layer and choose the servo's position before committing to the case.

<Entry date="TBD" title="Fabrication · Light Through the Form">

A cardboard box let us test the whole mechanism. We cut a slot in its side to hold the servo, keeping the horn outside so it could pull the wire toward the flower.

The enclosure then evolved into a round shell. Its flowing openings echo the petals and let the warm light spill out, connecting the form to the sunset theme. A removable lid gives access to the mechanism; a rigid bridge supports the flower and LEDs.

The milky sheet still revealed the wiring, so we added a thin translucent paper layer, similar to tracing or baking paper. This softened the transmitted light and hid the electronics behind a more even surface.

<Entry date="2026-10-15" title="Testing · Isolate, Then Reconnect">

The servo moved intermittently, and the LEDs were not responding reliably. I tested them separately so I could tell a wiring fault from a control or linkage problem. The servo commands appeared in Serial Monitor, but moving a wire changed its response—so I checked the connections first.

I spread out the crowded power and ground connections, repaired the LED wiring, then tested the two sticks on their own. Once the lights responded, I reconnected the servo and finally the flower linkage. Separate 5 V power for the loads and a shared ground kept the control wiring clear.

<Entry date="2026-10-15" title="Control · Forecast and Motion">

Hold translates the sunset-quality score into a still opening. During roughly a week of testing, the new forecast samples mostly stayed below 60. Using the full 0–100 range made those everyday differences less visible, so we mapped 60 to full opening. This makes the score-to-motion relationship clearer in a demonstration; scores above 60 share the same maximum.

Breathe demonstrates motion independently of the score. It shook at the highest point, so we reduced only its upper limit to 150°, keeping Hold’s mapping intact. Each movement takes three seconds, followed by a 1.5-second pause. Color follows forecast quality in both modes, and the light brightens as the flower opens.

<Entry date="2026-10-15" title="Interface · Reading the Forecast">

We built the frontend to make the connection between today's sunset quality and the flower's movement easier to read. The score sits beside an animated flower, so a visitor can relate the number to its opening and light at a glance.

Hold shows the forecast as a still position; Breathe demonstrates the movement; Stop ends the output. The webpage illustrates the commanded motion. This view shows our recorded October 15 forecast of 39/100.
