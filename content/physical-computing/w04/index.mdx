<Entry date="TBD" title="Concept · A Physical Forecast">

Sunset Flower turns Berkeley's sunset-quality forecast into movement and light. A positional MG90S servo moves a wire linkage to open the petals, while two RGB NeoPixel sticks illuminate the flower. The aim is to make a number noticeable as a small physical change.

The Adafruit Feather ESP32 V2 controls the outputs. After direct HTTPS requests on the campus network failed, the data request moved to a Python service on the Mac, which sends a validated forecast over USB. This separated internet access from physical control; the browser is only a supporting control panel.

<Entry date="TBD" title="Motion · From Paper to Petals">

We first tested a two-layer paper flower by hand. The layers meet near the petal tips; changing the distance between their centers changes the opening. Cutouts and thinner stems let the lower layer bend.

We tried a sideways pull before turning the flower upward, with the servo below and to one side. Testing the motion before designing the case helped us choose the orientation and see how the linkage moved the petals.

<Entry date="TBD" title="Fabrication · Housing the Motion">

A cardboard box held the mechanism while we tested the whole movement. We then developed the housing from a square box into a round case with curved openings, and widened it to make room for the components. The openings echo the petals and let light through.

The selected 199 mm housing has a removable lid for access. A rigid bridge supports the flower and LED sticks; the paper diffuser only spreads light. This separates structural support from the soft lighting surface. The final model set also includes two 0.38 mm petal sheets.

<Entry date="2026-10-15" title="Testing · Isolate, Then Reconnect">

To check the MG90S independently, I disconnected the LEDs and flower linkage and used short pulse tests, followed by a repeating sweep. Serial output confirmed that commands were running, but the motor sometimes stopped; moving a wire could make it work again. That pointed to an intermittent connection rather than a missing command.

I redistributed the crowded power and ground connections, repaired the LED wiring and tested the sticks on their own. After the light test worked, I added the servo back and confirmed that both outputs moved together. Separating these tests helped me distinguish software, wiring and mechanical problems.

<Entry date="2026-10-15" title="Control · Forecast and Motion">

Hold sets the opening from the sunset forecast, reaching its maximum command at 60/100. Breathe is a separate motion demonstration: it cycles through 5–150° regardless of the score. Light color follows forecast quality in both modes, while brightness changes with the opening.

Hold was stable, but Breathe shook at its highest point. Reducing only Breathe's upper limit to 150° kept the demonstrated motion working; Hold retained its data mapping. The final pacing is three seconds per movement and a 1.5-second pause. I learned to test each mode against the physical linkage, rather than treating one angle range as suitable for everything.
